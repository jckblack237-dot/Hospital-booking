The queue has no digital source of truth

A patient books for 16:00, arrives at 15:45, and is seen at 18:20. Nobody lied to them. The doctor started 40 minutes late, three walk-ins were inserted, and one consultation ran 25 minutes over. The clinic has no way to tell them any of this, because the queue lives on a paper register and in the receptionist's head.

The lobby is an information system

People sit there because being physically present is the only way to know their position. Crowding, anxiety and a receptionist answering "how long more?" all the way down.

An atoll trip is a whole day

A 20-minute delay in Malé costs an atoll patient a missed ferry and a night in a relative's flat. They cannot absorb uncertainty the way a Malé office worker can.

Nobody can see the pattern

No clinic owner can tell you which of their doctors habitually starts late, or that a 10-minute slot for a 14-minute consultation is what actually causes the backlog.

For clinics

The operational brain of the clinic

Receptionist Command Center

One board merging walk-ins, phone bookings and app bookings. Drag-and-drop reordering, configurable delay penalties, one-tap Viber/SMS dispatch, and a command palette so it can be run entirely from the keyboard.

Doctor module

One patient, two buttons. Start and End feed the wait-time engine, which is what makes every estimate real rather than theoretical. Punctuality is shown to the doctor first, framed as a scheduling input — never a leaderboard.

Billing & insurance

Multi-payer by default: Aasandha, private insurers, corporate accounts and self-pay, with split billing as the normal case. Claim construction, a rejection worklist, and rejection analytics. Payment rails via BML and m-Faisaa.

Admin & analytics

Doctor punctuality, daily revenue, patient volume — and a weekly digest written as findings, not a chart dump. "Dr. Hassan's median consultation is 14 minutes but he is scheduled at 10."

The hard part

The Dynamic Token Engine

A patient will leave the lobby only if they believe the number. They will believe it roughly twice before deciding it is noise.

Durations are log-normal

A hard floor, a dense mode, and a long right tail. Modelling the mean is the mistake that makes the people at the back of the queue systematically late.

Overruns don't go negative

For the patient currently in the room we use the conditional expected residual, not expected − elapsed. A consultation already running 20 minutes is evidence of a complex case — so the estimate grows, as it should.

Prayer pauses are in the model

Blackout intervals are first-class objects. An engine that treats a session as one continuous block is visibly wrong five times a day here.

A window, never a point

Patients see a range with a stated confidence. A single time is read as a promise, and every broken promise costs more than the vagueness of a range.

Leave early, on purpose

"Leave now" fires against the early edge of the window. Ten minutes early costs a waiting room; ten minutes late can cost the turn — and, for a ferry passenger, the day.

Silence is a bug

Every ETA change carries a reason. "Running 17 min later — a consultation ahead of you ran long" reads as a clinic being a clinic. A number that changes on its own reads as incompetence.

For patients

Wait at a café, not in a lobby

Find the right doctor

By specialty, by island, by language spoken, by whether they take Aasandha. Symptom search routes to a kind of doctor — never to a diagnosis — and intercepts emergency symptoms before anything else.

Watch the queue live

Which token is in the room, how many people are ahead, a time window you can plan around, and a push that says leave now.

Carry your paperwork

ID, Aasandha status, and GP referral letters in one place, available offline — because the referral letter you left on the island is a wasted ferry trip.

One account, whole household

Book and track for your children and your parents. In this market a single adult routinely manages care for three or four people.

Pricing

Priced per doctor. Reception seats are free.

Charging per receptionist would push clinics to share logins, which destroys the audit trail and the per-staff analytics. Never price a metric you need to be accurate.

Platform

We publish the API. Including to our competitors.

It removes the strongest objection in the sale — "I don't want to lock my patients into one app" — and rails outlast destinations. The clinic decides which partners can read its queue and how much of each session they may sell, and revocation takes effect immediately.
